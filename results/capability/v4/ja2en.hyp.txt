It takes another step to gender equality
Last year, the World Economic Forum predicted that it would take five generations to achieve gender equality in all countries. But the World Bank wants to cut that period in half.
The International Monetary Fund is launching a strategy to significantly advance the rights and economic opportunities of women by 2030. The plan places a heavy emphasis on increasing inclusivity and resilience through the use of financial services, technological innovation, reform of public policy and law.
The draft agreement says, "Recent research has shown that social norms and thought patterns can be changed. For example, it has been found that engaging men can increase support and endorsement for gender equality. "By understanding deeply how social norms and thought patterns shape expectations, priorities, and actions in life, we can accelerate the positive impact of gender on society and spread it."
It is difficult to quantify the change in thinking, such as calculating the number of bank loans to women in rural areas or the enrollment rate of girls in school. But there are many examples of how women's power can move mountains. In Iran, for example, the lack of women's willingness to comply with laws that force them to hide their hair has become the biggest threat to the Islamic government in the past 40 years. In some countries, such as India, where male resistance to women's voting rights and political activity is growing, there are very few women in parliament.
In parts of Africa, civic groups are turning harmful rituals into health education. One of the programs that helped change the mindset of a Kenyan, Mparasha Masai village chief, Kamanda Timayo, was about women's radicalization of the procedure. He told the Le Monde newspaper, "I want to get rid of this practice from ignorance."
The World Bank also cites other examples. Between 1970 and 2022, Bangladesh saw literacy and job creation rates double. The country's GDP tripled, and the change was driven by the fact that teachers and health workers from the community were hired on a full-time basis. Trust levels increased rapidly, and "women's activities and work are more likely to be accepted in society."
The World Bank's plan will still focus on norms and institutions that require a transition. In Rwanda, it took more than 20 years to establish a model case for land reform to improve women's economic status. In 2015, President Paul Kagame, who called for land reform, said, "We must change not only the law, but also thought patterns."

Determining which aircraft type is safest is not easy. The National Transportation Safety Board maintains a comprehensive database of accidents and incidents. The Federal Aviation Administration also provides a list of accidents and incidents, including those from the past 10 days, on its Air Safety Information and Sharing Page, which lists the manufacturer of each aircraft.
Another useful source of information related to the Federal Aviation Administration is the Boeing World Statistics on Commercial Jet Accidents 1959-2022, which classifies accidents by aircraft type and evaluates the safety of each type. In this report, which was released in September 2023, Boeing 787 and Airbus A350 are the least likely to be in a total loss, or a state of being unusable, compared to other aircraft types. The report also includes some aircraft types that have never experienced a total loss, such as the Airbus A380, which is a two-story, massive aircraft. The Boeing 737 MAX series, which has been in service since 2017, has an all-accident rate of 1.48 per flight.
