Women’s equality is still a long way to go. The World Economic Forum last year predicted that it would take five generations to achieve gender equality in all countries. But the World Bank wants to shorten that period considerably. The World Bank is developing a strategy to accelerate progress towards gender equality in women’s rights and economic opportunities by 2030, with a focus on inclusivity and resilience through financial services, technological innovation, public policy and legal reform. The plan’s draft proposal reads, “Recent research has shown that social norms and thinking patterns can be changed. For example, by involving men in gender equality, we can increase support and endorsement of gender equality.” Quantifying the change in thought patterns is more difficult than calculating the amount of bank loans to women in the local area or the percentage of girls in school. But there are many examples of women driving change. In Iran, for example, women who do not follow the law to hide their hair have become the biggest threat to the government in 40 years. In some countries, such as India, where women’s voting rights and political activity are being eroded by men, the Bank is hoping that this message will spread.
