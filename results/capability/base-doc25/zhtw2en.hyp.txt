This initiative also helps transform the old Gartshore Works site into land for redevelopment. The Scottish recycling enterprise that has processed over one million tonnes of construction, demolition, and excavation waste has opened its second factory after investing millions of pounds.

At Brewster Brothers' washing plant, they have fully converted their former construction industry operations—specifically excavation and gravel—to high-value granules for reuse. Over the past six years, the facility located near Linton, South Lothian, Scotland, has prevented nearly 13.5 million tonnes of construction, demolition, and excavation waste from entering landfill sites. It has generated more than 1 million tonnes of recycled granules sold to the industry.

In this company's expansion investment exceeding £60 million, Brewster reconstructed the old Gartshore Works facility nearby and now operates as a second recycling point. The new site will serve construction sites and housing developments across the western Midlands of Scotland. Currently staffed by 39 employees, it continues to provide sustainable waste management services and granule recycling to enterprises in Edinburgh, Fife, and Loch Ness.

Scott Brewster, General Manager at Brewster Brothers, who co-founded the company with his father Alex in 2017, stated: "It is increasingly clear that the construction sector in Scotland must reduce both the natural resource usage and carbon emissions associated with the industry while simultaneously increasing the use of recycling and secondary materials. Over the past six years, the volume of construction, demolition, and excavation waste recycled has grown significantly; our investment in this second factory point reflects further opportunities within this field."

Scotland's commitment to transitioning toward a circular economy is vast, and government planning to levy new granule taxes should enable faster reduction in primary product consumption. During the renovation of the deteriorated Gartshore Works facility, the company recruited new staff funded by the Green Employment Fund of the Scottish Enterprise Board. The company will also recover coal mine waste from the site, which will be transformed into local community parks.

Political figures misunderstand the limits on rent; when they encounter situations where their influence is expected, they instinctively intervene. However, if there were clear and simple solutions to the problem, those who understand it would have already taken action.

Consequently, we launched in approximately eighteen months ago the *Scottish Living Costs Act (Renter Protection)*, attempting to set rent ceilings within a limited period to alleviate pressure on living costs. Recent data from the latest Scottish government statistics reveals that, over the past year, average rents across all-scale properties have risen: one-bedroom units increased by 11.7%, while two-bedrooms reached a peak of 14.3%. Since October 2022, when the Living Costs Act was enacted, rents for all types of properties—from single rooms up to four bedrooms—have risen by roughly double the annual increase since then.

Related stakeholders warned extensively about this possibility but failed to address it seriously. They believed market forces dominated price rather than government intervention, leading to significant rent hikes. If such an industry had engaged in deep understanding, negotiation, and discussion, along with establishing principles based on supply and demand, this situation could have been avoided.

Following the end of the Living Costs Act on March 31, 2024, a new consultation document extended regulatory control over rent increases and allowed for future levies in the following year. This consultation concluded last week and proposed setting rent increases at the lowest level among three comparative factors: open-market rents, rents proposed by landlords, and the "reasonable" growth rate calculated using a newly designed system comparing current rates against market rates.

If experts' advice were ignored, tenants in Scotland might face increasingly worrying rent rises. The proposal suggests setting rent increases between 6% and a maximum of 15%. Notably, this ceiling exceeds the previous year's annual price increase. More importantly, monitoring and implementation of this scheme are difficult, overly complex, and costly. We already have a system involving primary housing arbitration bodies to regulate whether rent increases are fair. Thus, it appears merely to add more layers of bureaucracy, which neither landlords nor tenants benefit from.

Unfortunately, this consultation document further attempts to impose controls on this industry. A simpler approach would be to restore normal market conditions without external regulation of rent tiers; doing so would result in rent increases falling below the rise seen in the last 18 months. To achieve long-term stability of rental prices, the government must encourage private rental industries (PRIs) to expand investment and growth, while providing subsidies to social housing to promote development. As PRIs invest more, the housing market creates greater stability. Furthermore, once both the PRIs and social housing markets see increased capacity, rents will gradually stabilize as demand meets supply and equilibrium is achieved. Only through adopting these measures can we begin to respond to the current housing crisis. Other approaches will not yield results.

Please restore oversight of the Washington State prison system, as it faces an imminent and critical crisis involving human life. As the chair of our Legislative Working Group, we are tasked with researching the state's prison system and proposing changes to help ensure prisons remain safer—a fact that is both alarming but unavoidable. Although we come from diverse backgrounds including prison management, civil rights advocacy, and those who have experienced incarceration within the prison system, all agree: Washington's prisons must be supervised by an independent body.

In 2022, over 130,000 people were sent to prison in Washington State, yet their post-prison information was remarkably scarce. The state had not established a statewide office responsible for overseeing prisons and reporting data to the public; only a portion of the information related to death during custody was available. Our findings demonstrate that immediate action is required. Even if Washington's population growth were included in the calculations, the prison mortality rate between 2000 and 2019 increased nearly threefold—more than sixteen times the national average. Washington has surpassed other states in the nation's prison mortality rankings, currently ranking fourth highest. This situation cannot continue.

To reverse the trend of prison deaths and address many other problems facing our prisons, difficult policy decisions may be necessary. However, establishing an independent oversight body for prisons is crucial for achieving safer and more humane systems. Proven evidence shows that such oversight systems enhance transparency and accountability across Washington and nationally. According to our statistics, at least 25 states maintain statewide offices responsible for overseeing prisons, and their prison mortality rates are significantly lower than Washington's.

What sets Washington apart compared to other states is that they recently established or expanded independent oversight bodies for state prisons, police, schools, and other entities. For institutions managing hundreds of thousands of people's health and safety, prisons stand out as notable exceptions. An independent prison oversight office can monitor prison conditions, meet with staff and inmates face-to-face, report on firsthand accounts, express concerns before injuries occur, collect and report data to assist administrative personnel, policymakers, and the public in understanding our prison system—and advocate for solutions based on data rather than ad hoc measures.

Oversight does not merely benefit incarcerated individuals. Our working group concluded that the oversight office should collaborate with the heavy-duty prison system to provide urgent assistance and recommendations, including sound policies aimed at improving employee safety and reducing recidivism rates.

Between 1981 and 1987, Washington State temporarily established a statewide prison oversight office, which the U.S. Department of Justice once promoted as a model for domestic systems. Unfortunately, despite opposition from prison administrators, civil society groups, and others, legislative bodies ultimately removed the prison oversight institution.

In areas where long-standing lack of transparency benefits the public, independent oversight will foster public trust and promote data-driven decision-making. We request that the Legislative Working Group's recommendations be codified into law—that is, restoring the independent oversight of local, county, and regional prisons in Washington. This matters profoundly.

Healy O'Connell actively advocates against incarceration and serves as the Executive Director of Beyond These Walls, a community organization and executive director of Washington's Beyond These Walls initiative.

Isen Franklin, a licensed attorney employed by Disability Rights Washington, provides defense counsel for incarcerated persons with disabilities.

How to know if you've landed on a Boeing 737 MAX?  
For most passengers, the aircraft type is not a deciding factor when booking flights. However, after a body part fell off on January 6th in Alaska Airlines' flight, most Boeing 737 MAX 9 jet engines were temporarily suspended from service in the United States. Consequently, some potential travelers may want to know which aircraft they are boarding and which models offer the safest options. The first question is relatively straightforward; however, the second one requires more investigation.  

Most aircraft types are listed in the reservation details for each flight. On Google Flights, this information appears within the flight details page, and it is typically displayed on individual airline's reservation pages. If an airline does not clearly mark its aircraft type on either of these platforms, Expert Flyer and Seat Guru aggregate flight information—including seat maps and aircraft types—on their respective websites.  

Determining which aircraft model offers the safest option is more complex. The National Transportation Safety Board maintains a comprehensive database of accident reports and events. The Federal Aviation Administration (FAA), in addition, provides preliminary accident and event reports for the past 10 days on its "Flight Safety Information Analysis and Sharing" page, including data on related manufacturers.  

Another useful resource linked by the FAA is a summary titled "Global Statistical Summary of Commercial Jet Engine Accidents at Boeing Company, 1959–2022," which breaks down accidents by aircraft type to assess safety levels. According to the report released in September 2023, two aircraft models—the Boeing 787 and Airbus A350—are the least likely to suffer total destruction during an incident, as the number of times they have been destroyed exceeds the repairable range. Other models that never experienced such total destruction include the Airbus A380, a double-decker giant airliner. However, these models have accumulated fewer than 1 million takeoff runs over the reporting period.  

The report also includes comparisons of the frequency of total destruction per million takeoffs, helping illustrate why certain models are more common or have longer histories. Among these, the Airbus A310 was introduced in 1983 and has the highest rate of total destruction among all commercial jet models operating under passenger service (occurring 2.53 times per 100,000 takeoffs). The A320 series (including A321 and A319neo) exhibits the lowest rate at 0.11. The Boeing 737 MAX series began operation in 2017 with a rate of 1.48 per 100,000 takeoffs.  

It is important to remember that while these rates are calculated based on millions of takeoffs, several aircraft models introduced earlier—such as the Boeing 727 from 1964—have operated for decades. As a result, they inevitably accumulate a much longer record of accidents compared to newer models.  

Representative media correspondent Perry Curbat, who spoke on behalf of Seattle-Tacoma International Airport, expressed in an email that the causes of the incident span everything from equipment failures to human error. While it is possible to determine the typical class of flight based on available data, assessing safety level becomes significantly more difficult. Although airlines can compare flight models using available information, the FAA advises against comparing them based on airline accident records. An administrative department report states: "There is no evidence currently supporting ranking of airlines based on their safety records."  

Although significant differences often exist between airline safety records at any given time point, the FAA attributes most discrepancies to the rarity and severity of air incidents. These differences tend to level out over time, but the FAA declined to comment further.

How did Betta Edoué become the scapegoat for an unsound political system? During President Bola Ahmed Adeyemi's tenure, within the Humanitarian Affairs and Poverty Alleviation Department, Betta Edoué has been accused of allegedly misappropriating 5.85 billion Naira from the National Social Investment Office account to fund a private account designated as "Poor People's Assistance Fund" under the Federal Government's Poverty Intervention Project, which she claims was instructed by her to transfer funds to AG Oluwato Egbuwafo's personal account. Following the exposure of this alleged instruction, Edoué faces widespread public opposition.

According to the leaked document dated December 20, 2023, Edoué instructed the National Social Investment Office account holder, Mr. Brijit Ouniyelu, who serves as an accountant in the Poverty Intervention Project, to move funds into his own private account; the project falls under his department. Media assistant Rashid Zubaier, representing Edoué, claimed that such conduct complies with Nigeria's framework for civil service services. However, it is noteworthy that this requirement violates multiple Nigerian laws enacted in 2009: these regulations explicitly prohibit government business fraud and other forms of corruption. Furthermore, Article 713 of Chapter VII of the 2009 Financial Regulations clearly stipulates that no personal funds shall be paid to any government bank account, nor should any public funds be paid to private accounts. Additionally, the regulations further define that any official who pays public funds to private accounts is deemed guilty of intent to defraud.

During an interview with *Investing Africa*, an anonymous senior legal officer from a major commercial bank commented on the matter: "This involves several issues. First, the head of the department is relatively new, and former head Sahida Umaru Farkouk, who previously served as the first creative director, had engaged in what appeared to be illegal activities. This may reflect the way operations are conducted within this department. However, paying public funds to private accounts violates procurement rules." He added that unless there is valid approval provided by such authorities, this action does not necessarily constitute fraud but remains incorrect and lacks certainty.

On Twitter, political and digital communications expert Akhen Awoniwai expressed views suggesting that while the head might not have violated the law, using a personal account could not be considered so. Legal analyst and development and management consultant Iyoabo Awokeya also expressed similar sentiments regarding the dismissal of the head, expressing sympathy. She argued that the head may have committed unintentional errors due to urgency in completing work.

Based on these experts' points, one can infer that federal governments and semi-state-owned enterprises hold differing opinions on financial and legal matters. Such divergence became more pronounced following the release of this leaked document, thereby making Betta Edoué a scapegoat for an unsound political system.
