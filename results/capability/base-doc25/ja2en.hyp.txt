Washington State has been reconstituting its prison surveillance system again, with the urgent concern that human rights issues remain at stake. The state's prison administration is in dire crisis conditions. As co-chair of the Special Committee on Justice tasked with studying and proposing reforms to enhance safety, I have faced this unavoidable reality: the prison management, civil liberties advocacy, and history of incarceration represent diverse backgrounds from which we all stand united by our position that independent oversight is required within Washington State prisons.

In 2022, over 130,000 individuals were incarcerated in Washington State prisons, yet little information exists about what occurred there. There is no statewide agency responsible for monitoring prisons or reporting to the public; only partial data regarding deaths during confinement is available. Our findings indicate that immediate action is necessary. Even accounting for population growth, between 2000 and 2009, the death rate in Washington State prisons increased nearly threefold compared to national averages—a 16-fold increase. This places Washington State among the top four states in U.S. prison mortality rates. It cannot be left unaddressed.

To reverse trends in prison mortality and address other pressing challenges facing prisons, difficult policy decisions will likely be required. However, independent oversight of prisons represents a crucial first step toward developing safe and humane prison systems. Independent oversight has become an established method across the nation to improve transparency and accountability. According to our calculations, there are at least 25 state agencies overseeing prisons nationwide, and their prison mortality rates are lower than those in Washington State.

On the ground, Washington State has recently introduced or expanded independent oversight mechanisms within state correctional facilities, law enforcement agencies, schools, and other organizations. Yet, as the sole subject of oversight, prisons manage the health and safety of thousands of people.

Independent prison oversight bodies can monitor inmate conditions, conduct face-to-face meetings with staff and inmates, report on situations they have personally witnessed, and alert authorities before incidents occur. They also collect and report data, enabling managers, policymakers, and the general public to understand the prison system and propose solutions based on data regarding prison deaths and related issues. Oversight does not apply only to prisoners; the Special Committee confirmed that it cooperates with prison oversight agencies to ensure adequate support and suggestions are made, including sound policies aimed at promoting staff safety and reducing recidivism rates.

Between 1981 and 1987, the state's overall prison oversight agencies were established, and the Department of Justice of the United States praised them as models across the country. However, the legislative branch opposed these efforts, leading to the abolition of state-level prison oversight. In such a field where long-term operation often yields limited benefits, public trust and data-driven decision-making are essential. We urge the legislature to revive independent oversight of prisons at local levels—cities, counties, and districts—and seek to codify our committee's recommendations. Human lives are at stake.

How to determine whether the aircraft in your cabin is a Boeing 737 MAX or another model?  
For most travelers, the specific type of aircraft does not affect flight reservation decisions. However, following an accident on Alaska Airlines Flight 1959 (January 6), many Boeing 737 MAX 9 jets have been put on hold domestically due to operational restrictions. Consequently, some passengers may be inclined to consider which aircraft they will fly and which one offers the highest safety profile. While determining the safest aircraft among others is relatively straightforward, it can be more complex for those who need to verify their own cabin configuration.  

Generally, aircraft models are listed in flight reservation information. On Google Flights, detailed flight details include the model number; individual airline reservation pages also display this data. If you cannot locate the correct model number through Google Flights or an airline's reservation page, specialized flight agents or seat gliders may provide such information via flight tables or models.  

Determining which aircraft offers the best safety record is not always easy. The Department of Transportation maintains comprehensive databases regarding accidents and incidents. The Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) has also released reports containing information about accidents involving manufacturers from various airlines over the past ten days, including those with significant damage or failure.  

Another useful source related to the FAA is the "Boeing World Statistics Overview: Commercial Jet Aircraft Accidents 1959–2022," which categorizes accidents by aircraft type and evaluates their safety levels. In September 2023, this report indicated that two types of aircraft—Boeing 787 and Airbus A350—experienced total loss, rendering them non-operational. Among these, the Boeing 787 had the lowest reported occurrence rate at 0.00% (all lost). Additionally, the report included several aircraft that had never suffered any total loss, such as the twin-story Airbus A380. However, the operating hours for these models were still below 1 million during the time of the report.  

The report also highlighted comparisons between the all-accident rates per 1 million operating hours across different models. Some models may have gained popularity or been used longer than others. For instance, the Airbus A310 introduced in 1983 remains operational as a passenger aircraft but carries the highest all-accident rate at 2.53 per 1 million operating hours. The lowest all-accident rate was observed among the Airbus A321 series, including the A319neo, at 0.11 per 1 million operating hours. The Boeing 737 MAX series, launched in 2017, currently exhibits an all-accident rate of 1.48 per 1 million operating hours.  

This report's data is calculated based on 1 million operating hours. It is important to note that while older aircraft like the Boeing 727 operated for decades before being replaced, they accumulated significantly more accident records compared to newer models.  

Perry Cooper, the Public Relations Officer at the Seattle Port Authority, stated via email that the cause of accidents often stems from equipment issues rather than human error. Therefore, while verifying the aircraft model in your cabin is relatively simple, assessing its safety level presents greater challenges.  

Based on available data, aircraft models can generally be compared, though the FAA advises against comparing aircraft solely on accident records. According to the FAA's comments, there is no basis yet to rank individual airlines based on safety records derived solely from accident data. The FAA noted that differences in airline safety records vary notably over time, particularly concerning catastrophic events with low frequency but devastating impacts. These distinctions tend to become uniform over time unless addressed explicitly.
