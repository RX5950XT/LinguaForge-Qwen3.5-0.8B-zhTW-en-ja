Please restore oversight of the state prison system in Washington because it is at risk to life. As co-chair of our legislative working group on the state prison system and proposing changes to help make prisons safer, we have been facing this crisis as one of the most alarming but unavoidable facts that we face. Although we come from different backgrounds including prison management, civil rights advocacy, and some who have experienced incarceration, we all agree: The state prison system must be overseen by an independent oversight body.
Over 130,000 people were incarcerated in the state prison system in 2022, yet very little information was available about their incarceration after they entered the system. There is no statewide office overseeing prisons and reporting data to the public, with only a portion of the information being related to death during incarceration.
Our findings demonstrate that immediate action is required. Even if you take into account population growth, the state prison mortality rate between 2000 and 2019 increased nearly three times higher than the national average, a 16-fold increase. This situation cannot continue.
To reverse the trend of prison deaths and address many other issues facing our prisons, difficult policy decisions may be necessary. But for a more safe and humane prison system, an independent oversight body is critical as the first step. Proven evidence shows that independent oversight systems can improve transparency and accountability both in Washington and nationally. According to our statistics, at least 25 states have statewide offices overseeing prisons, and their prison mortality rates are lower than those in Washington.
In contrast to us, the state recently established or expanded oversight bodies for state prisons, police, schools and other entities. Prisons are notable exceptions to oversight of health and safety institutions serving millions of people.
An independent prison oversight office can monitor prisons, interview staff and inmates, report on firsthand accounts, even express concerns before injuries occur. An oversight office can also collect and report data to assist administrators, policymakers and the public in understanding our prison system and promote data-driven approaches to solving prison deaths and other issues associated with prisons.
Oversight is not just good for prisoners. Our working group determined that oversight offices can work collaboratively with overburdened prison systems to provide the assistance and advice needed, including smart policies that improve employee safety and reduce recidivism.
The state of Washington had briefly created a statewide prison oversight office between 1981 and 1987, which the Department of Justice once touted as a model for domestic oversight. Sadly, despite opposition from prison managers, civil society organizations and others, legislators removed oversight bodies.
In areas where long-standing lack of transparency benefits the public, independent oversight will foster trust and data-driven decision-making. We call on legislators to adopt the working group’s recommendations as law, restoring oversight of city, county and regional prisons. It matters.

How to know if you’re on a Boeing 737 Max
For most passengers, the type of aircraft is not a deciding factor in booking a flight. But after the fuselage parts fell off on January 6th at Alaska Airlines, most Boeing 737 Max 9s were banned from flying in the United States, so some potential travelers may want to know what kind of plane they’re on and which types are safest. The first question is easy to answer, but the second one is more complicated.
The type of aircraft is usually noted in the boarding information for each flight. On Google Flights, this information is included in the flight details when expanding the list, and it’s often listed on individual airline’s booking pages. If an airline doesn’t clearly mark the type of aircraft on their Google Flight or booking page, websites like Expert Flyer and Seat Guru aggregate flight information, such as seat maps and aircraft types.
Determining which type of aircraft is safest can be more complicated. The National Transportation Safety Board maintains a comprehensive database of accidents and incidents. The Federal Aviation Administration also provides preliminary accident reports and related manufacturer data on its “Airline Safety Information Analysis and Sharing” page during the past 10 days.
Another useful resource linked by the FAA is the “Boeing Commercial Jet Accident Global Statistics Summary, 1959–2022,” which breaks down accidents by aircraft type to assess safety. As of September 2023, the report notes that the Boeing 787 and Airbus A350 are the least likely to experience total loss of all components (the aircraft was beyond repair), while other types have never experienced a total loss, including the double-decker Airbus A380. However, these types had fewer than 1 million takeoffs during the reporting period.
The report also includes statistics on how many times per million take-offs each type of aircraft has experienced a total loss, which helps explain why certain types are more common or longer-lived. The Airbus A310, introduced in 1983, has the highest rate of total loss among commercial jet models (2.53 per million take-offs). The Airbus 320 series (including A321 and A319neo) has the lowest rate, at 0.11. The Boeing 737 Max series began service in 2017 with a rate of 1.48.
It’s worth remembering that even though these rates are calculated per million take-offs, some of the aircraft types reported in the report (such as the 1964 Boeing 727) have been in operation for decades, meaning there will always be a longer time elapsed before accumulating accident records.
Seattle-Tacoma International Airport’s media representative, Perry Cooper, wrote in an email that the causes of accidents range from equipment failures to human error. While it’s possible to easily determine the type of flight being taken, determining safety levels based on this information is much more complex.
While it’s possible to compare aircraft types using available data, the FAA does not recommend trying to make comparisons based on airline accident records. According to an administrative departmental report, “There is no evidence currently in the accident record to support any airline ranking based on safety records.
Although airline safety records vary significantly over specified time periods, the FAA states that most of these differences are due to ‘the rarity and severity of air accidents,’ and those significant variations between airlines tend to level out over time. The FAA declined to comment.
